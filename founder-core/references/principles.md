# Founder OS — Operating Principles

These are the constraints every Founder OS skill inherits. Every `SKILL.md` in the marketplace points here. When a skill's advice and a principle conflict, the principle wins.

## A1. Experience gaps, not ideas

You do not "find" a business idea. You mine your own history for places where you were the frustrated expert: something you had to do repeatedly, badly tooled, with domain knowledge that took years to earn. The moat is that the problem is hard to understand from the outside. Ideas that anyone could have had are ideas anyone can copy.

Test: *Could a smart generalist with a weekend and ChatGPT build this?* If yes, the gap is too shallow.

## A2. Validation is evidence, not opinion

Compliments are worthless. Only three things count as validation: **money** (pre-orders, deposits, paid pilots), **time** (someone using a manual/ugly version repeatedly), or **access** (someone introducing you to their peers unprompted). Every validation skill must report which of the three it produced.

## A3. Sell before you build, build before you polish

Order of operations: offer → landing page → conversations/pre-sales → concierge MVP (you do it by hand) → productized MVP → automation. Skipping steps is the most common way to spend six months building something nobody buys.

## A4. Revenue is the only PMF metric that can't lie to you

Retention and referrals are the leading indicators; revenue that recurs without discounts is the confirmation. Vanity metrics (signups, followers, waitlist size) are inputs, never outputs.

## A5. Processize before you delegate, delegate before you hire

Never hire for a job you haven't written the SOP for. Never automate a process you haven't run manually at least ten times. Sequence: do it → document it → delegate it → automate it → delete it if possible.

## A6. Sustainable > fast

Default to profitability. Fundraising is a tool for a specific class of business (winner-take-most markets, heavy upfront capex), not a milestone. The $1M target here is annual revenue run-rate, reached without external capital unless the unit economics genuinely require it.

## A7. Minimalism as a discipline

Every quarter, run the Minimalist Review (skill 30): what can be deleted — features, channels, tools, meetings, people-hours, expenses. Complexity compounds faster than revenue.

## A8. Decide fast on reversible things, slow on irreversible ones

Most founder decisions are two-way doors. Spend deliberation budget only on one-way doors: pricing model, ICP, co-founder, fundraising, first hire.

## A9. Constraints are inputs

Every skill starts by asking for the founder's constraints: hours/week available, runway in months, risk tolerance, whether this must replace a salary and by when. A side-founder with 15 hours/week gets a different plan than a full-time founder with 18 months of runway. Skills must adapt, not assume.

## A10. Write it down or it didn't happen

Every skill produces a file. Decisions get a one-paragraph decision log entry. This creates the institutional memory that lets later skills (and later hires) work without the founder in the room.
