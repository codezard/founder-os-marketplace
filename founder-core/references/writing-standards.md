# Founder OS — Writing Standards

Every customer-facing word a Founder OS skill produces (copy, pages, emails, decks, outreach) follows these. Internal artifacts follow the plain-text and specificity rules too.

## Specificity Rule

Every claim gets a number, a name, or a time. "Saves you time" → "Cuts month-end close from 3 days to 4 hours." Abstractions get replaced with the customer's own concrete phrases (from `founder/evidence-ledger.csv` and the `words_they_use` field).

## One-CTA Rule

One asset, one action. A page, email, or message asks the reader to do exactly one thing. Competing CTAs split attention and lower conversion.

## Plain-text-first

Write as if the message were plain text with no design. If it doesn't work as plain text, design won't save it. Applies to emails especially — plain-text-first, image-light, one idea per message.

## Banned words

Do not use, and flag if the founder asks for them: **revolutionary, seamless, leverage, unlock, empower, cutting-edge, game-changer, synergy, world-class, next-generation, robust, best-in-class, disruptive.** They carry no information and signal AI-generated copy. Replace each with a specific claim.

## Edit Pass

Before delivering any copy: cut adverbs, replace abstractions with concrete customer phrases, one idea per sentence, read-aloud test. If a persona member cannot understand the offer in 5 seconds, cut again.

## Awareness levels

Match where the copy starts to the reader's awareness: unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → most-aware. Cold audiences need the problem framed first; warm audiences want the offer.

## Proof hierarchy

Specific number > named quote > anonymous quote > adjective. Reach for the strongest proof available before dropping down.

## No fabrication

Never invent market numbers, community sizes, competitor prices, testimonials, or customer quotes. Source them or mark them explicitly as assumptions. When web search is available, use it; when not, say so and leave a gap for the founder to fill.
